# New disclosure rules for automated decision systems

The regulator has published final rules requiring operators of automated
decision systems to disclose, on request, the main factors behind an individual
decision. The rules take effect twelve months after publication and apply to
systems used in credit, insurance, housing, and employment.

## Scope

The rules cover a system that produces a decision, or a score used to make a
decision, with no meaningful human review. A system that only assists a human who
retains genuine discretion is out of scope, though the operator must be able to
show that the discretion is real.

Small operators below a turnover threshold have a further six months to comply.
The threshold is fixed in the rules and is not indexed to inflation.

## What must be disclosed

On a valid request from an affected person, the operator must provide:

1. The categories of input data the system used.
2. The main factors that drove the specific decision, in plain language.
3. Whether the person can request human review, and how.
4. The retention period for the decision record.

The operator need not disclose the model's weights, its training data, or a
factor-by-factor numeric breakdown. The standard is a meaningful explanation, not
full technical transparency.

## Enforcement

The regulator may audit an operator on notice and may require a compliance plan
where it finds gaps. Financial penalties are reserved for repeated or wilful
failures. There is no private right of action; enforcement is exclusively by the
regulator.

## Open questions

Industry groups have asked for guidance on how the plain-language standard
applies to ensemble models, and on whether a single explanation can cover a batch
of similar decisions. The regulator has said guidance will follow before the
rules take effect.
